# Figure alt text

- `e1_main_night.png`: Dot plots of P(feasible), P(within 1 % of optimum) and expected-cost ratio on the main night (Oct 8 to 9, 16 qubits), comparing the two untrained start states with QAOA using the X mixer (blue circles) and the XY mixer (vermilion squares) at p = 1 to 3, one dot per seed plus median and interquartile range. XY QAOA at p = 3 puts 0.086 probability on schedules within 1 % of the optimum, 39 times its start state (0.0022), and raises the expected-cost ratio from 0.64 to 0.77.
- `e2_savings_all_nights.png`: Smart charging saves a median 13 % of charging cost and 15 % of CO₂ on windy nights. Left and middle: one point per night against the carbon swing (log axis), CO₂ and cost saved versus charge-on-arrival for 362 nights; windy nights are orange filled circles, calm nights hollow sky-blue squares, and savings grow with the swing. Right: median CO₂ saving per month as hatched green bars with interquartile whiskers, and the number of windy nights per month as an orange line.
- `e3_penalty_vs_xy.png`: The XY mixer samples feasible schedules 84 % of the time, against 62 % for the penalty X mixer (p = 3, ideal simulation). Two stacked panels, probability of a feasible sample (top) and expected-cost ratio (bottom), for the X mixer at p = 1 to 3 and two larger penalties and for the XY mixer at p = 1 to 3, with two-qubit gate counts under each variant. Filled circles are ideal simulation, hollow squares an approximate IonQ-like noise model; blue is the X mixer, vermilion the XY mixer; dashed and dotted lines mark the untrained start states.
- `e4_angle_transfer.png`: Train once, schedule every night: XY angles trained on the main night reach twice the near-optimal probability of the untrained start (median 0.039 against 0.019 over 28 other nights; training on each night gives 0.061). Four scatter panels, one point per night against the carbon swing (log axis), with the X mixer in the top row and the XY mixer in the bottom row; left, expected-cost ratio, right, probability of a sample within 1 % of the optimum. Grey diamonds are the untrained start, black circles per-night training, orange triangles angles reused from the main night, sky-blue squares angles reused from a calm night.
- `e5_pareto.png`: One weight w trades cost for CO₂: at w = 1 no night emits more than charge-on-arrival. (a) Electricity cost against CO₂ for all 500 feasible schedules of the main night (grey points) with the Pareto staircase, exact optima for w = 0 to 1 (green circles), charge-on-arrival (black cross), the median schedule (grey diamond) and XY-QAOA samples (vermilion triangles); at w = 1 the optimum emits 15.7 kg CO₂ against 38.1 kg. (b) and (c) CO₂ and cost saved versus charge-on-arrival as a function of w over 362 nights, median and interquartile band, windy nights orange with solid line, calm nights sky blue with dashed line. (d) Share of nights where the optimum emits more CO₂ than charge-on-arrival, falling to zero at w = 1.
- `e6_scaling.png`: At 16 qubits the XY mixer samples feasible schedules 85 % of the time, against 62 % for the X mixer. Four line panels over 11, 16 and 20 qubits (fleets of 2, 3 and 4 EVs): probability of a feasible sample, probability of a sample within 1 % of the optimum (log axis), expected-cost ratio and two-qubit gate count. Hollow blue markers are the X mixer, filled vermilion markers the XY mixer, marker shape and line style give the depth p = 1, 2, 3, and black crosses show the untrained start states. At 20 qubits both mixers sample about 10 % feasible schedules.
- `e7_depot_year.png`: Smart charging saves 252 t CO₂ and €124,000 over a year on the same grid connection, for a 100-bus depot over 362 nights of Dutch grid data (carbon-first schedule, 5.0 % less CO₂ and 8.2 % lower energy cost than unmanaged charging). (a) and (b) Hatched green bars of CO₂ and wholesale energy cost saved per month, with savings in every month. (c) Smallest grid connection that works: 6.9 MW unmanaged, 5.4 MW with first-come-first-served capping, 3.3 MW with the smart schedule.
- `e8_hybrid_depot.png`: QAOA subsolvers inside the 100-bus depot close 97 to 99 % of the CO₂ gap, like exact enumeration. (a) and (b) Depot CO₂ in kg against the index of the large-neighbourhood-search subproblem on the windy main night and on a calm night, one line per subsolver (exact enumeration solid green, QAOA XY dashed vermilion, QAOA X dotted blue), with the first-come-first-served start as a dash-dotted line and the MILP reference as a dotted grey line. (c) Seconds per subproblem on a log axis: exact enumeration takes under a millisecond, simulated QAOA a few seconds.
- `main_demo_2026-10-01.png`: The same layout for the calm night of Oct 1 to 2: carbon intensity bars with the price as a dashed line, and EV-by-block grids for charge-on-arrival and the optimal schedule. On this night the optimal schedule saves little CO₂ against charge-on-arrival (-1.4 %).
- `main_demo.png`: top, bar chart of carbon intensity per 2-hour block with the day-ahead price as a dashed line with markers; below, two EV-by-block grids (charge-on-arrival versus the optimal schedule) in which each EV has its own fill, hatch and marker (A blue circle, B orange crosshatched square, C green dotted triangle), white cells are available but idle and grey cells are not plugged in; the optimal schedule moves charging to the low-carbon blocks near the end of the night.
